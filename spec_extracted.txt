2. Voice and tone
SAVI's entire product promise is that it does not overstate, guess, or bluff. The website must sound the same way. Every word on the site should be something that could be defended in a room full of chartered accountants.
The voice is
Plain-spoken. Short sentences. No jargon where an ordinary word exists.
Precise. Specific claims, not atmospheric ones. “Every number traces to its source” rather than “unparalleled accuracy”.
Calm and confident. It does not shout, use exclamation marks, or oversell.
Respectful of the reader. The audience is senior finance professionals. They should never feel talked down to.
The voice is not
Hype-driven. Banned words and phrases: revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, industry-leading, seamless, unlock, supercharge, 10x.
Vague. Every adjective must be doing real work. If a sentence still reads correctly with the adjective removed, remove it.
Fear-based. Do not sell by frightening the reader about fraud or penalties.
Hard rules on claims
No statistic, percentage, accuracy figure, benchmark result, client count, or performance number may appear anywhere on the website unless Saakshi has explicitly supplied and approved that exact figure in writing.
No client names, logos, testimonials, case studies, or quotes — none exist yet, and placeholders must not be used.
No security certification claims of any kind. See Section 12.
No award, ranking, analyst mention, or press quote.
No feature described as available unless it is confirmed available. Where a capability is planned, either omit it or label it clearly as planned.

5.2 The problem
Section heading
The data is already there. The trust isn't.
Body
Finance teams at large organisations are not short of data. They are short of answers they can act on without verifying them first. Reports arrive late. Numbers disagree between systems. And the new generation of AI tools makes this worse, not better — because a model that generates a figure can generate a wrong one just as fluently as a right one.
A number you have to check by hand is not intelligence. It is more work.

Nothing is estimated. If the data isn't there, SAVI tells you that instead of filling the gap.

5.5 Built for complexity
Section heading
Multiple entities. Multiple jurisdictions. One clear picture.
Body
Most finance tools decide which rules apply to a company by looking at a single country field. Real organisations are not that simple. A company incorporated in one country, operating in three others, owned by a parent in a fifth, and reporting under a sixth standard has four different sets of obligations at once — and getting that wrong is where cross-border finance breaks down.
SAVI resolves the rules for every entity from four dimensions at once: where it is incorporated, where it operates, who owns it, and who it reports to. Each resolved rule traces back to the specific fact that triggered it.

10. Who It's For
Page heading
Built for finance teams carrying real complexity.
Intro
SAVI is most valuable where the finance picture is genuinely hard to assemble — several entities, more than one country, and a team that spends more time reconciling numbers than acting on them.
Groups with overseas subsidiaries
A parent company in one country with subsidiaries in others faces several sets of rules at once, and a consolidated picture that has to be rebuilt by hand every period. SAVI resolves the applicable rules per entity and traces every consolidated figure back to the entity and record it came from.
Multi-entity organisations
Where each entity has its own ledger, its own filings, and its own reporting calendar, the group view is usually the last thing to arrive and the first thing to be questioned. SAVI keeps the entity detail and the group picture connected, so any consolidated number opens into its components.
Finance teams under audit pressure
When every reported figure must be defensible, the ability to open a number and show its origin changes the nature of the conversation. SAVI records the lineage as the number is produced, not afterwards.
Organisations evaluating AI for finance
Most AI tools in this space generate figures, which means they can generate wrong ones convincingly. SAVI is architected the other way round: the calculation is deterministic and traceable, and the AI is used only to explain what was computed. If you have been asked to assess AI for the finance function and have concerns about reliability, this is the distinction worth examining.

The four steps

The trace, in full
Show as a horizontal or vertical chain graphic:
Figure → Formula → Query → Rows → Source record
Each element should be visually distinct and connected in sequence. This graphic is the single most important visual on the website.
Why this matters
A general-purpose AI model produces text that sounds correct. When the subject is language, that is usually good enough. When the subject is a number in a set of accounts, it is not — because a fluent wrong figure is more dangerous than an obvious one. It gets believed, and it gets acted on.
SAVI separates the two jobs. The computation is deterministic and reproducible. The AI writes the explanation. That separation is why a figure from SAVI can be checked, and why the answer to “where did this come from?” is always a record, never a guess.
When SAVI doesn't know
If the underlying data is missing or insufficient, SAVI reports that plainly instead of producing an estimate. An honest gap is more useful than a confident approximation — and considerably safer.

How SAVI handles your data.
Intro
SAVI is designed to operate inside your environment, on infrastructure you control. Your financial records are yours, they stay yours, and they are never pooled, shared, or reused.
Data sovereignty
SAVI is designed to run within your own infrastructure — your financial data does not need to leave your environment.
Your data is never used to train models for any other organisation.
Your data is never pooled with, or shared between, clients.
Access control
Role-based permissions govern what each user can see and do.
Every request to the system is authenticated.
Repeated failed sign-in attempts result in the account being locked.
Auditability
Every change to business data is recorded with its before and after values.
System access and request activity is logged separately from business data changes.
Audit records are immutable — they cannot be edited or removed after the fact.
Data integrity
Accounting entries are validated at the point they are written; unbalanced data is rejected rather than partially accepted.
Imported files are validated in full before any records are created.
Every reported figure retains its lineage to the source records it was computed from.
Ethics and responsible AI
Our commitment
SAVI is built on a single principle: it must never be confidently wrong. In financial reporting, a plausible incorrect figure causes more damage than no figure at all, because it is trusted and acted upon. Every design decision in SAVI follows from this.
What this means in practice
Numbers are computed from your records. The AI describes results — it does not produce figures.
Where data is missing or insufficient, SAVI states this rather than estimating.
Every figure retains a traceable path to its source records.
SAVI supports financial decision-making. It does not replace professional judgement, and it is not a substitute for advice from a qualified accountant, auditor, or legal adviser.

16. Intellectual property protection — mandatory
This section is not optional. Read it before building any page. SAVI's competitive advantage lies in how it is built, and the website must market the product without disclosing how it works.
16.1 What must never appear on the website
Product screenshots showing real interface detail, data structures, field names, table layouts, menu structures, or system messages.
Architecture diagrams, data-flow diagrams, database schema, or entity-relationship diagrams of any kind.
Names of the specific technologies, frameworks, databases, libraries, AI models, or hosting infrastructure SAVI is built on.
Code samples, query examples, configuration files, or API endpoints.
API documentation, developer documentation, or integration guides — these are supplied to clients under agreement, never published.
Descriptions of how the traceability mechanism is implemented internally. Describe what it does for the user; never how it is constructed.
Benchmark methodology, test-question sets, accuracy-measurement approach, or evaluation criteria.
Internal terminology, module names, or component names used in the product.
Any file, document, or specification not explicitly intended for public release — including this document.
16.2 Product visuals — the rule
The site needs to feel like a real product without handing over the blueprint. The rule is simple: show the outcome, never the mechanism.
Use abstract or simplified illustrations rather than real interface captures.
Where an interface impression is needed, use a stylised representation with invented sample values, generic labels, and no real structural detail.
Never use a real company's data, a real client's data, or genuine internal figures — even blurred or partially obscured.
All numbers appearing in any graphic must be clearly invented and, where sensible, labelled as illustrative.
Any product visual must be approved by Saakshi before it goes live. If in doubt, leave it out and use an abstract graphic instead.
16.3 Legal protections to put in place
These sit outside the website build but protect what the website exposes. Flagged here so they are not overlooked.
Trademark registration for the SAVI name and the logo mark. This is the strongest practical protection for brand identity and should be filed before significant public promotion. Saakshi to take advice on the correct classes.
Copyright notice in the footer of every page (wording given in Section 4).
Terms of Use for the website, explicitly prohibiting scraping, automated data collection, reverse engineering, and reproduction of site content.
